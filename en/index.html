Low is a cautious assumption with slow diffusion, Mid an intermediate case and High rapid diffusion where AI agents take over whole process steps. The scenarios are not forecasts and are not probability-weighted. Freed-up capacity beyond the shortfall assumes that labour can move to the occupations where the shortfall is. The range in the last row depends on whether the calculation uses 46 or 147 occupational groups.

Conclusion

Agentic AI can free up more labour capacity than is needed to cover the shortfall – in all three scenarios. Beyond the shortfall, this corresponds to 261,000 to 921,000 employees in 2035.

But the capacity is freed mainly in office, casework and expert occupations, while the shortfall is in health and social care, construction and services. There, the own effect is not always enough: in the cautious scenario a shortfall of 48,000–74,000 people remains if no one changes occupation. Transition and movement of labour between sectors therefore becomes the decisive question. Otherwise Sweden could face a shortage and a surplus at the same time.

Why scenarios and not a forecast

Agentic AI only began to see wider use during 2026. Measured effects to date concern almost exclusively generative AI used as a chat interface. They show no broad transformation of employment yet, though a weakened entry path into AI-exposed occupations for young people is visible. The effect levels are therefore the author’s own assumptions, stated and justified openly. The investigation recommends updating the calculation every six months as outcomes become measurable.

What it means for policy

  1. Direct AI specifically at the shortage occupations, above all health and social care, and measure the effect.
  2. Make occupational and sectoral mobility possible at a scale today’s systems are not built for, and make use of people outside the workforce.
  3. Only if the freed capacity is still not absorbed do working hours, retirement age and the tax base become the central questions. They need preparation now, but decisions should depend on what the monitoring shows.

What the investigation cannot say

How fast this unfolds, how much of the freed capacity will be absorbed by new demand and new tasks, and whether the required mobility is achievable. The main calculation assumes no volume compensation and is therefore an upper bound; even at 50% compensation, 89% of the Mid-scenario shortfall is still covered.
